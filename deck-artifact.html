The Ask

Build an editorial platform people choose to spend time with.

  • An evolving editorial platform about the relationship between spaces and the lives lived within them.
  • Stories about people, experiences and everyday life — not about projects.
  • An exploration of how design quietly shapes how we feel, live, connect and belong.
  • A universe built across culture, food, people, cities, homes, hospitality and nature.
  • An independent identity of its own.
  • A platform that becomes distinctive, loved and culturally relevant over time.

Not another page about spaces.
A platform about what spaces make possible.

The brief restated in the client's own terms, but arranged editorially — asymmetric two-column, headline left, obligations right, and one closing line that reframes the whole ask. Note the pivot: "page about spaces" → "platform about what spaces make possible". Everything after this slide is an answer to that sentence.

Original Microdrama

Sometimes you rent an apartment.
Sometimes you find a home.

A Design Zariya Original.

Hard cut. Full-bleed powder-blue wash crossfades through, the title settles line by line, and there is no synopsis — the point is emotional curiosity, not information. The thin rust rule is one of only three places the client's existing terracotta identity is quoted in the deck.

The Thought

Everyone, somewhere, is trying to build a home.

For some, home is the place they were born in — the one they carry with them long after they leave it.

For some, it is a place they move to. A city chosen for work, or for distance, or for a fresh start.

For some, home is a person.

And for some, it is the people they find along the way — the ones nobody planned for.

A home is ultimately a place where we belong.

Nesties' emotional premise, stated before any character appears. The pacing here is deliberately the slowest in the Foundation-to-Chapter-One handover: longer stagger, softer dissolve. We want the audience to arrive at "belong" on their own.

Nesties

Two girls. One apartment. A home they never planned to build together.

The only place Caveat appears in the entire system — and only on the word "Nesties". Loose, handwritten, young, imperfect; never elegant, never calligraphic, never bridal. The hero frame does the introduction: the contrast between the two women should be legible before a single line of biography.

Her first home that is completely hers.

Window motif, stage two of four (10 → 12 → 13 → 16). This is its most aspirational appearance — generous sky, warm light, an almost empty room that reads as possibility rather than emptiness. Slide 13 will reuse the same framing and let the light do the damage.

Stories

No tagline, no description, no synopsis. A masthead and a rule. Restraint is the entire point — this chapter is about looking, so the opener asks the room to look at one word.

The Thought

A city is never just one place.

It is the places everybody knows, and the places nobody talks about.

The places it has forgotten, and the ones it has hidden.

The extraordinary, and the entirely ordinary.

And in every one of them, people — the ones who give a place its life.

Together, they tell the story of a city.

Chapter Two changes the motion language: slides now push horizontally rather than dissolve, and images drift slowly (Ken Burns) so frames feel alive rather than placed. Pastel is used far more sparingly here — the photography carries the colour.

What is Stories?

A documentary series about the places that make a city, and the people who make those places matter.

  • The Known.
  • The Unknown.
  • The Hidden.
  • The Forgotten.
  • The Special.
  • The Everyday.

A celebration of the many stories that collectively build the identity of a city.

Pure typography. Six words, each landing on its own beat, built to be read aloud. The list is the format brief for the whole series — every future episode has to sit under one of these six words.

Four ways of looking at a city

01

Known

The places a city is proud of. They appear in every photograph, and are still rarely understood.

02

Unknown

The places that exist in plain sight, and have simply never been looked at closely.

03

Hidden

The places you have to be taken to. Behind a door, above a shop, inside a courtyard.

04

Forgotten

The places a city has stopped noticing. Not necessarily abandoned. Not necessarily ruined. Simply no longer looked at.

Every city exists in all four at once.

Note the correction built into 04: forgotten does not mean abandoned or in ruin. A functioning shop can be forgotten; a busy lane can be forgotten. That distinction is what keeps the series from becoming ruin photography.

Stories is as much about people as it is about places.

Seven sentences, one subject: somebody. The repetition is intentional and should be read slowly — it is the casting brief for the series as much as it is copy.

Deliberately territories, not locations. No real place is named and no fictional one is invented — the moment the deck names a specific address it stops being a format and starts being an episode we would have to defend. Locations get chosen with the client, on recce.

Beyond the space.

Elements — A Design Zariya Original.

Third and final hard cut, this time through sage. The signature is set very small on purpose: the chapter is about noticing quiet things, so it introduces itself quietly.

The Thought

A space is never just what we see.

  • Colour changes our mood.
  • Sound changes our state of mind.
  • Greenery changes how alive it feels.
  • Energy changes how naturally we move through it.
  • Organisation changes how clearly we think inside it.

Some of the most powerful elements of a space cannot be measured in square feet.

Chapter Three's motion is crisper and slightly faster than the other two — an editorial journal rather than a film. The five lines arrive at a quicker cadence because this chapter is about noticing many things, not dwelling on one.

Elements

The forces that make a space feel like more than a space.

An expert-led series exploring the things that quietly shape how a space feels — colour, sound, nature, energy and order — through the people who understand them best.

What makes a space feel right?

The property is named and defined in two lines, then hands straight to a question. Elements is the most "useful" of the three ideas, so it earns its place by being immediately practical rather than aspirational.

What makes a space feel right?

Calm? Warmth? Energy? Balance? Personality? Breath?

That is where Elements begins.

Six words, six question marks, enormous negative space. Read aloud in a room this slide becomes a pause — which is exactly the tempo change the deck needs before the format slides.

The Format

One element. One expert. One new way to see a space.

Each episode hands a single element to a specialist who has spent their life with it, and lets them explain it the way they would to a friend — accessible, visually rich, genuinely useful.

Expertise translated into everyday experience.

Who we'd bring in

  • Colour specialists
  • Interior designers
  • Sound & music experts
  • Landscape & plant experts
  • Feng Shui & Qi specialists
  • Professional organisers
  • Wellness specialists
Butter is the expert world's accent and it appears only on this slide and its neighbours. The expert list is a casting territory, not a booking — no names are claimed here.
01

Colour

Azure. Crimson. Ochre. Sage.

What a colour does to a room — and to the people inside it. Each episode takes one colour and follows it through a space with a colour specialist and an interior designer.

02

The Sounds of Serenity

Music that soothes spaces.

What a home sounds like when nobody is speaking. With musicians and sound designers, on the difference between silence and quiet.

03

Breathing Spaces

Indoor plants.

What changes in a home when something living moves in. With landscape and plant experts, on light, care and the plants that actually survive us.

Three distinct editorial worlds, not three equal cards — different widths, different accents, different micro-motion. Colour gets a slow wash pulse, Breathing Spaces gets a barely-there sway. The rule that only one accent may lead per slide is respected by keeping each episode's colour inside its own block.
04

Energy Flow

"It's not about size. It's about the Qi of a space."

With Feng Shui and Qi specialists — why some rooms are easy to move through and others quietly resist you, and what can be changed without changing anything structural.

05

Less is More

Confessions of a Professional Organiser.

With professional organisers — what a cluttered room is actually telling you, and how much lighter a space feels when a single shelf is emptied.

Two larger blocks so the rhythm breaks from Slide 32 rather than repeating it. The organiser frame gets a gentle scale-and-settle on entry — the motion itself should feel like a room exhaling.

Design doesn't end when the room is finished.

It continues in

  • a colour
  • a plant
  • a song
  • a ritual
  • a rearranged chair
  • an emptied shelf
  • a different way of moving

Elements takes the philosophy of thoughtful spaces and makes it useful, accessible and participatory. It doesn't set out to teach anyone architecture. It helps people notice their own spaces differently.

Look again.
Your space is already talking to you.

The third chapter argument, again with no strategy label. This is also the most commercially useful slide in the deck: it turns Design Zariya from something the audience watches into something they can do on a Sunday afternoon in their own home.
